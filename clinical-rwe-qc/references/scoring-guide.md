# Scoring guide

These operational rules follow the item numbering of the 2024 quality-control guidance for real-world studies in clinical comprehensive drug evaluation. Consult the [guideline](https://doi.org/10.12458/HDPR.202408073) and its [commentary](https://doi.org/10.12458/HDPR.202408074) for the source instrument. The table describes how to assess reporting. The numerical score records support in the report.

## Study objectives

| Item | Criterion | Scoring boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Operational research question | Look for a defined population, exposure or comparison, measurable outcomes and a feasible data source or method. A budget line is unnecessary when feasibility is evident from the design. |
| 2 | Explicit study objective | Use the reported clinical or policy question and evidence gap to judge whether the objective is clear and relevant. The paper need not use a specific phrase about novelty. |
| 3 | Testable hypothesis | Look for a stated expectation or relationship that could be tested, including a directional, equivalence or noninferiority hypothesis. A comparative objective by itself is insufficient. |

## Study design

| Item | Criterion | Scoring boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Suitability of source population | Identify the real-world records or participants and assess fit with the target population and setting. A single site can be suitable. Judge sample size justification separately under item 9. |
| 5 | Eligibility criteria | Give credit for explicit, clinically coherent and actionable inclusion and exclusion rules. The absence of a committee sign-off is insufficient grounds for 0. Look for contradictions or missing rules that prevent application. |
| 6 | Exposure and outcome definitions | The report should identify the actual exposures, comparators and measurable outcomes well enough to understand the comparison. Definitions taken only from a cited trial cannot establish how the current study measured outcomes. |
| 7 | Covariates and bias strategy | Look for covariates linked to plausible sources of bias and a design or analysis to address them. A baseline significance test alone does not address confounding. Assess time-related mechanisms where relevant. |
| 8 | Index date and alignment | Look for the start of observation or exposure for each person or group and assess time alignment. A calendar window does not define an individual index date. |
| 9 | Sample size justification | Look for a calculation appropriate to the design and endpoint, with relevant assumptions such as the target effect, precision or power. Observed counts alone are insufficient. |
| 10 | Follow-up period | Determine whether follow-up is defined and suitable for the outcomes. Recruitment dates alone are insufficient. A period sufficient for an acute endpoint may be too short for longer-term safety. |

## Data sources and preprocessing

| Item | Criterion | Scoring boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | Description of data sources | Identify institutions or datasets, observation periods and retrieval methods. For linked datasets, check how sources were matched and harmonized. |
| 12 | Fit between data and question | Check that the source can represent the relevant population, exposures and outcomes and that use of the data is traceable. Assess cleaning, verification and governance in their own items. |
| 13 | Relevance of captured data | Connect the variables actually collected and their timing to the intended outcomes. For linked sources, check whether linkage supports that connection. |
| 14 | Data collection procedures | Look for extraction tools, fields or rules and a concrete practice such as piloting, review or monitoring. A database name or job title alone is insufficient. |
| 15 | Data cleaning | Look for reported operations such as duplicate removal, consistency checks, format harmonization or handling of outliers. A software name is insufficient. |
| 16 | Data verification | Look for source-record checks or a distinct consistency verification process. Cleaning the dataset or checking baseline balance does not establish independent verification. |
| 17 | Data management plan | Check for a description of handling, storage, authorized access, privacy or quality controls. Ethics approval by itself does not establish these processes. |
| 18 | Consent and privacy protections | Use reported consent, applicable waiver or concrete confidentiality measures with the ethics information. Describe exactly what was reported. Ethics approval alone does not establish consent handling or protection measures. |

## Statistical analysis

| Item | Criterion | Scoring boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | Statistical analysis plan | Look for a planned strategy that addresses the design, outcomes, applicable confounding, and relevant missingness or subgroup analyses. A named or registered document is unnecessary if planning is evident. A list of tests is insufficient. |
| 20 | Treatment effect analysis | Check that the model or comparison addresses the stated effect, outcome type and relevant intercurrent events or censoring. A list of tests or an unadjusted comparison alone does not establish a suitable analysis. |
| 21 | Methods for confounding control | Credit an explicitly implemented adjustment or matching strategy with identifiable covariates. Make clear when the method handles measured covariates only. |
| 22 | Overadjustment for mediators | Look for assessment of variables on the causal pathway. Criteria for ordinary prediction-model selection are insufficient unless used to address this issue. |
| 23 | Subgroup analyses | Look for a defined subgroup based on a prognostic factor or effect modifier and a corresponding analysis. Treatment arms or a demographics table alone are insufficient. |
| 24 | Differential measurement or classification | Check for methods such as standardizing measurement or calibrating procedures across sites or groups. Baseline balance after matching does not establish comparable measurement. |
| 25 | Reverse causation | Check whether the timing or design addresses the possibility that the outcome influences exposure. Matching alone does not establish temporal ordering. |
| 26 | Missing or misclassified data | Look for the extent of the problem and a corresponding action, such as documented exclusions, imputation or correction. Silence does not establish completeness. |
| 27 | Multiplicity | Look for an implemented correction or a defined testing hierarchy. A usual significance threshold alone is insufficient. |
| 28 | Sensitivity analyses | Credit a fitting robustness check conducted in the report. An economic model using the study's real-world data can qualify. A planned check or one borrowed from a citation is insufficient. |

## Results and interpretation

| Item | Criterion | Scoring boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 29 | Reporting completeness | Check that the question, design, data, analysis, main findings and conclusion of the real-world component can be followed. A missing specialized detail need not invalidate overall completeness. |
| 30 | Participant accounting | Look for the flow from screening to inclusion and follow-up, with numbers and relevant reasons by stage or group. The information can be in text, a table or a diagram. |
| 31 | Descriptive and main results | Check for participant characteristics and the main results appropriate to the design and outcomes. Only require measures relevant to that design. |
| 32 | Interpretation and limitations | Look for interpretation of the findings and material limitations specific to the current study. A generic statement acknowledging limitations is insufficient. |
| 33 | Transferability | Look for a connection between findings and an identifiable target setting or population, with limits tied to the study design or data. A standalone statement that use should be expanded is insufficient. |
| 34 | Research translation | The study's scoring convention accepted identifiable public dissemination paired with a specific intended clinical or policy use. State what was documented and avoid inferring adoption, implementation impact or changes to guidelines. |
| 35 | Disclosure of interests | Under the study's minimum criterion, an identifiable funding or financial disclosure can support credit for transparency. State the scope of that disclosure. It does not establish the absence of other interests. An explicit competing-interest declaration also provides evidence. |
| 36 | Access to protocol, data or code | Look for a stated way to obtain a protocol, dataset or code, including an explicit request process. An email address or access to the published report is insufficient by itself. |

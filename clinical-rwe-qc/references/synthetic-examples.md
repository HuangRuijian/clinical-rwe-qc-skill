# Synthetic decision examples

These illustrations use invented reports. Read the relevant item in the scoring guide alongside an example. The examples illustrate specific evidence thresholds and do not set the expected frequency of positive scores.

| Item | Evidence that can support a score of 1 | Evidence that is insufficient on its own |
|---|---|---|
| 3. Testable hypothesis | “We hypothesized that the generic formulation would meet the prespecified noninferiority margin for the 30-day endpoint.” | “We compared the two formulations.” |
| 5. Eligibility criteria | “We included adults with a first outpatient prescription and at least 180 days of prior records. We excluded concurrent use of both formulations.” | “Records with suitable clinical information were selected.” |
| 8. Index date | “Day zero was the first eligible dispensing date in both treatment groups.” | “Data were drawn from January through December.” |
| 15 and 16. Cleaning and verification | “We removed duplicate encounters before linkage.” supports 15. “A second extractor compared each selected record with the original chart.” supports 16. | “All data were entered in a spreadsheet.” |
| 18. Consent and privacy | “The institutional review board waived consent for secondary use of records. Study files used coded identifiers.” This supports the reported waiver and coding. | “The study received ethics approval.” |
| 19 and 20. Planning and treatment effects | “Before extraction, we specified the primary endpoint and a weighted model using baseline disease severity and prior use.” Consider 19 in light of the full plan, and 20 in light of the effect, outcome and model. | “We used a t test, a chi-square test and a significance threshold of 0.05.” |
| 21 and 25. Confounding and time order | “We weighted by baseline severity, age and prior admissions.” supports handling measured confounding under 21. An explicit washout, alignment or lag to address treatment triggered by early signs of an outcome can support 25. | Weighting alone does not establish either removal of unmeasured confounding or control of reverse causation. |
| 28. Sensitivity analysis | “The 30-day analysis was repeated using a 14-day window. The effect direction was unchanged.” | “The authors of a cited trial reported several sensitivity analyses.” |
| 34. Research translation | An identifiable disseminated report explicitly intended to inform a local formulary reassessment can support documented dissemination and intended use. | A general aspiration without a specific dissemination or use pathway does not establish translation. |
| 35. Disclosure | “The report was supported by the fictional Regional Research Council.” Record that funding was disclosed. | An author’s departmental affiliation alone does not disclose an interest. |
| 36. Access | “The analysis code is available from the study team upon reasonable request.” | A correspondence address alone does not establish access. |
